Big News

A rebuilt ArcHoops is coming

We've finished a top-to-bottom redesign of the student experience—lessons, profile, settings, leaderboard, and points, plus a rebuilt admin experience for schools. It's in staging now and launching soon.

The new leaderboard is the biggest visible change. Students can sort by XP, accuracy, or streak—so someone who isn't leading on raw points can still see where their consistency stands—and it now defaults to a student's own class rather than the whole school.

Product Update

Rewarding reasoning, not luck

Most prediction games have the same flaw: a student who guesses randomly and gets lucky scores the same as one who studied the matchup and reasoned carefully. That teaches the wrong lesson.

So we're changing how points work. The new model gives partial credit for well-reasoned predictions—even when the game doesn't go that way. Sound statistical reasoning becomes the thing that pays off, not the coin flip.

Scouting report, front and center

We're also moving the data to where the decision happens. The scouting report is coming to the top of the prediction view, defaulting to a histogram of a team's scoring distribution with a dot plot below it—plus historical season data.

  • See the shape of the data before you pick, not after
  • Spot clusters, spread, and typical vs. unusual outcomes at a glance
  • Practice the same displays students meet in their math standards

A small interface change with a big instructional payoff: students build the habit of reading a distribution before making a call. In our Park Forest pilot, students who completed at least one lesson predicted with 15% higher accuracy.

Built For Classrooms

Student privacy, by design

If ArcHoops is going to live in classrooms with students under 13, privacy can't be an afterthought. Several of the changes shipping with the redesign exist specifically to protect the students using it.

What's changing

  • Usernames hidden on public leaderboards—students still see their own standing and compete with classmates, without identifying details exposed school-wide
  • A dedicated sign-up flow for under-18 users, built around state requirements including New York, California, and Louisiana
  • A formal privacy policy covering how student data is handled and secured

For parents, the competitive part kids love doesn't come at the cost of your child's information. For district leaders and consultants evaluating us, COPPA compliance is built into the product, not bolted on later.
