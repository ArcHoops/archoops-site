Thank you for being here. This edition covers what we've built for schools and families alike, a simpler way students meet the data, and an open question we're working through about how good thinking should be rewarded.

Big News

Putting the data where the decision happens

The scouting report is moving to the top of the prediction view. Instead of picking first and seeing the numbers after, students will open a matchup to a clear visual snapshot of a team's scoring patterns and recent history—before they make the call.

Scouting Report — Team Snapshot
[ Scouting report mock-up screenshot ]

Why it matters

  • See how a team has really been playing before you pick, not after
  • Spot patterns and surprises at a glance
  • Put to work the same data-reading skills students build in our lessons

Reading a scouting report well is its own skill, and it's one our lessons teach directly—walking students through how to spot trends, spread, and what actually tends to predict a win. Moving the report into the moment of decision gives students a place to put that skill to work right when it counts.

The payoff shows up in the numbers: in our Park Forest pilot, students who completed at least one lesson predicted with 15% higher accuracy than those who hadn't.

Product Update

Built to fit your classroom—or your living room

Last issue showed you the new student dashboard. This one is about what surrounds it: whether ArcHoops shows up as a full school rollout or as something a family signs up for at home, getting started should be simple either way.

For schools, the redesigned admin experience handles the parts that used to slow things down—organization management, bulk email invitations, and account rules that keep enrollment limited to a school's own accounts. A teacher or district administrator can bring in a full roster in one pass instead of chasing individual sign-ups.

Admin Dashboard

Teachers also get separate teacher and assistant-administrator roles and can toggle between a single classroom and a school-wide view. And for families signing up on their own—no school or district required—we've built parental consent right into the flow: a student can dive in right away, but a parent or guardian gets an email to approve the account, and if we don't hear back within a set window, the account and everything in it is automatically deleted. It's privacy built in from the first click, not bolted on after. It's all in staging now and launching soon.

Two more pieces of that: usernames are now hidden on public leaderboards, so students see their own standing and compete with classmates without exposing identifying details, and we've published a formal privacy policy covering how student data is handled and secured.

What's Next

On deck

We're designing a badging system so students have milestones to chase beyond the leaderboard, finishing the interactive lessons, and getting the redesign into classrooms ahead of the coming season.

We're also chewing on a bigger question. In most prediction games, a student who guesses at random and gets lucky scores exactly the same as one who studied the matchup and reasoned it out—which quietly teaches the wrong lesson. We're exploring an idea: giving kids some credit for a well-reasoned prediction, even on the nights it doesn't pan out. It's still an open question for us, not something on the roadmap yet. If you're an educator, coach, or parent with thoughts on this, we'd love to hear them—info@archoops.net.
