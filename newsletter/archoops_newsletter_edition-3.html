The season starts Oct 20. So does your first prediction.

Thank you for being here. This edition is about the public launch, a shout-out to the first classroom to call ArcHoops home, and what's next for both the kids playing at the kitchen table and the ones playing as a class.

School Spotlight

Meet our Founding Classroom

Every new thing needs a first believer. For ArcHoops, that's Park Forest, the class that spent last season testing predictions, lessons, and more than a few bugs so this launch could be ready for everyone else. We're proud to call them our Founding Classroom.

Opening Night Classroom Challenge

On October 20, classes everywhere can predict the score, the winner, and a team stat for opening night—then review the results together the next morning. It's a fun way for a class to kick off the season as a team, not just as individual players.

Any class or school that joins before Thanksgiving earns the same Founding Classroom badge Park Forest has. If that's you—or you're a teacher who wants it to be—info@archoops.net and we'll get you set up.

Product Update

Smoother for everyone, start to finish

A lot changed behind the scenes to get ready for a full season of real games. A few of the upgrades you'll notice:

What's new

  • A global leaderboard for anyone, class or no class—direct sign-ups now have somewhere to compete from day one
  • Interactive lessons that actually track your progress, so finishing one unlocks the next without a second thought
  • A toggle to follow NBA and WNBA games side by side once both seasons are underway
  • A feedback button right in the app—tell us what's working (or isn't) without leaving the page

We'll keep calling this a young, fast-moving release—expect a few rough edges this season as we keep tuning it with real games and real students.

Built For Every Student

One sign-up experience, for every age

We simplified how we protect students using ArcHoops. Instead of different rules for different ages, every student under 18 now gets the same experience: they can start predicting and using lessons right away, while a parent or guardian is asked by email to approve the account. If we don't hear back in time, the account and its data are automatically deleted—no action needed on your end.

Usernames stay hidden on every leaderboard, so classmates can compete without anyone's identity on display. Nothing here requires a school or district to set up—it works the same whether a student joins through a classroom or signs up with a parent at home.

What's Next

On deck

Last issue, we asked whether a prediction game could reward good thinking, not just a lucky outcome. We're starting to answer that: watch for Student Analyst of the Month, a monthly shout-out for the class's best-reasoned prediction—not just the correct one.

We're also building a Founding Educator program for a handful of teachers who want early access to new features and a direct line to give feedback, plus Family Data Night kits for schools that want to bring parents and kids together to predict as a group.
